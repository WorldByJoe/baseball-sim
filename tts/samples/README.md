# Voice samples

`tts/samples/README.md · v0.1 · 2026-10-02`

**Listen first, then choose one voice per role in `../voices.json`.** Every candidate says the same five lines, each at the pace and energy the broadcast would give it, through the speech server's own processing (`render_samples.py`; the `pa` file has the ballpark echo):

| file | role · energy · pace | the line |
|---|---|---|
| `_colour` | colour · calm · 1.30 | Kendrick can run: twenty-nine feet a second, faster than nine players in ten. |
| `_tension` | play-by-play · building · 1.00 | Two and two. He comes set. |
| `_action` | play-by-play · excited · 0.92 | Line drive to right-center! Into the gap! Quinlan is into second with a double. |
| `_homerun` | play-by-play · peak · 0.88 | Swung on, a high drive to deep left... Get out of here! Gone! |
| `_pa` | public address · calm · 1.25 | Now batting for the Comets, the catcher, Darwin Rodríguez. |

(The broadcast now paces the PA at 1.05; the samples were made at the first plan, 1.25.) MP3, mono, 40 kbps.

**Render speed** is the real-time factor on this cloud machine (4 cores, idle; seconds of rendering per second of speech, the voice already loaded). On the TV's Pi 5, Ryan medium measured 0.25-0.32 against 0.07 here, so expect about four times these figures there. The game speaks about 38 s a minute (call_check.js), so a voice needs a Pi factor well under 1 to stay ahead: the Piper medium voices (about 0.3 on the Pi) do; the Piper high voices (about 1.1) and Kokoro (0.5 here with the full model, so about 2 on the Pi by the same ratio; the tts README's earlier figure was 0.5 there) would fall behind over a whole game and suit only short pre-rendered lines. **To be measured on the Pi.**

**Licences** are from each voice's model card (the licence of the recordings it was trained on). The project is private and non-commercial, which the NC licences allow; the CC0, public-domain and Apache voices carry no such limit.

## Suggested defaults (`../voices.json`)

| role | voice | why |
|---|---|---|
| `pbp` | Piper `en_US-ryan-medium` | the voice already used on the Pi for the radio call; quick to render; clear at speed |
| `colour` | Piper `en_US-norman-medium` | a different, deeper male voice for the slow colour; public domain |
| `pa` | Piper `en_US-kristin-medium` | a female voice, unmistakably not the booth, with the ballpark echo; public domain |

If the NC licence of Ryan matters, `en_US-joe-medium` (CC0) is the play-by-play alternative. Kokoro's voices are the more natural ones to many ears (try `am_michael`, `am_onyx` and `bm_george`), but they render too slowly on the Pi for a whole game's live call.
