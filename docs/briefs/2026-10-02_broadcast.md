## What this project is (read before touching anything)
A trait-driven, physics-based baseball simulation. Every statistic emerges from players' traits, physics and noise. The screen, `baseball.html`, plays one whole game on a fixed schedule: the engine simulates the whole game first (about 50 ms), then the page REPLAYS it segment by segment - `pregame`, then per half `halfStart`, per at-bat `change` (a pitching change or pinch hitter, when there is one) and `paStart`, per pitch `setup`, `flight` and `result`, then `halfEnd`, and finally `final`. Durations are in `PACE`; the schedule is the array `SEG` (each segment has `kind`, `t0`, `dur`, and `play`/`i`/`inn`). A game runs about 50 minutes at 1x. A game is fixed by its seed: the same seed is the same game, pitch for pitch. The page also runs on a TV wall from `file:///home/joe/tv_art/` in the kiosk's Chromium on a Raspberry Pi 5, which already allows sound without a click.

Read, in this order: `README.md`; `sounds/SOUNDS.md` (how the crowd behaves and every sound to collect - this is the design, follow it); `tts/README.md` (Piper on the Pi, measured speeds and limits); `baseball.html` from the top through the schedule builder (`add(...)`, `playDuration`, `segAt`), then `render()` and its lane banners, `drawThink`, `paWords`, `dayOf`, `drawOverlay`, `winProb` and where each play gets `wpBefore`/`wpAfter` (the home team's chance of winning); `bb_game.js` for what a `play` record carries (`desc`, `pa.result`, `runs`, `basesAfter`, `outsAfter`, `score`, `newPitcher`, `pinchHit`, and the pitch records with `result`, `pitch.type`, `pitch.mph`, `call`, `steal`, `wild`, `bb`).

## The design (decided with the owner)
- **The script is written ahead.** At load, once the game is simulated, the page writes the whole broadcast: every voice line, every sound cue, the crowd's state. Nothing is improvised during play.
- **Everything is keyed to an event ID.** Every segment gets a stable, readable ID built from the game, not from its position in `SEG`: for example `7b.3.4.flight` (bottom of the 7th, third batter of the half, fourth pitch, the flight), `7b.3.up` for the walk to the plate, `7b.start`, `7b.end`, `pre`, `final`. A cue names its event ID, an offset in seconds from that segment's start, and how long it may last before the next cue needs the air.
- **Prepare ahead, play on the moment.** The voice for each line is rendered well before its moment and played exactly when its segment begins (plus its offset). A line is never played late: if its audio is not ready, it is skipped.
- **Slow colour, fast play-by-play.** Colour commentary (the batter's notable traits, how he did in earlier at-bats, how the game is going) is spoken slowly in the gaps before a pitch; the play-by-play is fast. Measured with Piper's Ryan voice: pace 1.3-1.45 with half-second pauses gave 125-180 words a minute; pace 0.92 with short pauses about 220.
- **Energy tags.** Every voice line carries an energy - `calm`, `building`, `excited`, `peak`, or `deflated` - not just a pace. Piper has no emotion control, so for Piper the energy becomes pace, sentence pauses, punctuation (short sentences and exclamation marks for the big calls), Piper's variability setting, and a small pitch lift and a little extra volume on `excited` and `peak`. (A pitch lift without changing speed: synthesise slightly slower, then resample up by the same factor.) A better engine could map the same tags to real emotion later without touching the script.
- **The booth leans home.** The broadcast is the home team's radio crew: excitement is larger for the home team's good moments, flatter for the visitors'. Every word stays true.
- **Free voices only, run on the Pi.** No paid services. The voices are rendered on the Pi, ahead of the picture, from the script.

## Phase 2 - the script writer
`bb_call.js`: pure JavaScript with no DOM, loaded after `bb_schedule.js`, deterministic from the game. Given `G` and `SEG` it returns the broadcast:
- `lines`: `{ id, event, offset, role, voice, text, pace, energy, maxDur }`. Roles: `pbp` (play-by-play), `colour`, `pa` (the public-address announcer: "Now batting, the left fielder, number..."; home batters, and the starting line-ups in the pregame).
- `sfx`: `{ event, offset, sound, gain, dur }` using the sound names in `sounds/SOUNDS.md` (field sounds timed to contact, catches, the wall, slides; crowd reactions timed to the resolution).
- `crowd`: per segment `{ event, arousal, valence, bed }`, computed as `sounds/SOUNDS.md` describes (stakes from the page's chance-of-winning estimate; reactions sized by how far a play moved it; missed calls from the pitch location against the rulebook zone).
- `organ`: `{ event, offset, cue }` following the organ table.
What to say: the batter coming up (name, position, how he has done today, a notable trait against the league); the count and the pitch in a few words; balls in play called as they happen, from the play's own description, the fielder, the runners and the score; home runs, steals, wild pitches, pitching changes, the end of each half, the final. Colour in the gaps: true observations from the data - bat speed against the league, a pitcher's pitch count and fatigue, the chance of winning, the park's altitude and the air.
**The timing budget**: each line's length is estimated from a table of measured seconds per word at each pace and energy (measure it in Phase 3 by rendering real lines), and must fit before the next cue that needs the air; shorten or drop what does not fit.
`headless/call_check.js` runs the writer over several whole games under Node and jsc and prints the broadcast against the schedule: each line, its event, estimated length, the time available, every line dropped or shortened, and the sound and organ cues. Report the share of at-bats with colour, the share of lines dropped, and the most crowded stretch.
