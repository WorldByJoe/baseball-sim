## The problem, in numbers
Measured the way Statcast measures it (exit velocity ≥ 80% of the most that bat speed and pitch speed could give: EV_max = q·v_pitch + (1+q)·v_bat), the model squares up 78% of contact; the 2025 league squares up 33.7% ± 4.1 across hitters. Consequently exit velocity averages 93.7 mph against 89.7 ± 2.2, and the hard-hit share (95+) is 0.53 against 0.42; yet EV50 (the top half of a hitter's exit velocities) is right, 100.7 vs 100.6. The best contact is right; the MISHITS are missing. Today contact is two-state: a pitch the batter "detected" is read almost perfectly (resid 0.05 in readFactors) and struck with a small, Gaussian scatter (barrelSD ≈ 0.6 in vertical, longSD ≈ 2.6 in along the barrel); a "fooled" pitch is misread by the whole ghost-vs-real separation. Real hitting has a continuous middle: late reads, partial adjustments, flares, topped balls. Related league-level symptoms (run_games): batted-ball mix GB/LD/FB/PU 49/17/20/14 vs MLB 43/24/24/9; HR% 1.7 vs 3.0; K% 26.8 vs 22.6 (don't make it worse); BABIP .294 vs .291 (fine). Also a correlation the contact model should start to produce: bat speed ~ squared-up rate is −0.52 across real hitters and −0.06 in the model (a harder swing is a less precise one; precision already worsens as bat speed squared, but the resulting mishits don't show because the scatter is too small to matter within the contact window).

## The mechanism — where to look, in physics first
The collision already converts an off-centre strike (vertical offset D, along-the-barrel offset dLong) into the right weak contact: a big D gives a pop-up or a topped ball with low exit speed, dLong off the sweet spot lowers q. So the task is to widen the DISTRIBUTION OF CONTACT OFFSETS without widening the WHIFF tail (|D| ≥ R_SUM = 2.75 in is a miss) — the added spread must live mostly inside ±2.75 in. Candidate mechanisms, in the order I'd try them:
1. Timing error feeding vertical offset through the swing and pitch planes (missing today: timing e only feeds the spray angle theta). The bat path rises at the attack angle and the pitch descends (~6–10° at the plate); an early/late bat meets the ball at a different point along both paths, so D should gain e × (v_bat·sin(attack) + v_pitch_vertical) — pure geometry, no new constants. Check the signs against the engine's conventions (e > 0 = bat early; D > 0 = ball above the barrel).
2. A continuous tracking error for detected pitches instead of the fixed resid 0.05: the batter's estimate of where the ball will be carries an error that grows with how late he resolved the pitch (separation at commit vs his spotting distance, and time-to-plate tp), so "detected" is a spectrum from fully read to barely read. Keep the fooled branch as it is.
3. Only if 1 and 2 are not enough: a heavier-shouldered motor scatter (a mixture of the precise swing and a "close but not on it" swing) — but this is the least physical of the three and needs a new trait, so justify it if you use it.
Implement one at a time, measure the full suite after each, keep what helps, delete what doesn't (no dead code, no commented-out code).
