## What this project is (read before touching anything)
A trait-driven, physics-based baseball simulation. Players have latent traits; every statistic emerges from traits, physics and noise. The screen, `baseball.html`, plays one whole game on a fixed schedule: the engine simulates the game first (about 50 ms), then the page REPLAYS it segment by segment - `pregame`, then per half `halfStart`, per at-bat `change` (a pitching change or pinch hitter, when there is one) and `paStart`, per pitch `setup`, `flight` and `result`, then `halfEnd`, and finally `final`. Durations are in `PACE`; the schedule is the array `SEG` (each segment has `kind`, `t0`, `dur`, and `play`/`i`/`inn` as relevant). A whole game runs about 50 minutes at 1x. The page also runs on a TV wall from `file:///home/joe/tv_art/` in Chromium.

Read, in this order: `README.md`; `tts/README.md` (the speech capacity, its measured speeds and its limits - this matters); `baseball.html` from the top through the schedule builder (`add(...)`, `playDuration`, `segAt`), then `render()` and the lane banners in it (the existing one-line descriptions of what just happened are the seed of the call), `drawThink`, `paWords`, `dayOf`, `drawOverlay`; `bb_game.js` for what a `play` record carries (`play.desc`, `play.pa.result`, `play.runs`, `play.basesAfter`, `play.outsAfter`, `play.score`, `play.newPitcher`, `play.pinchHit`, the pitch records with `result`, `pitch.type`, `pitch.mph`, `steal`, `wild`).

## What to build
1. **`tts/speech_server.py`** - the bridge, following the wall's existing pattern (`tts/README.md`, "The game page cannot run Python"): a standard-library HTTP server on `127.0.0.1` only, its own port (8898 unless you find a reason not to), `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *` on every response including the OPTIONS preflight. It loads one Piper voice once at start (configurable; default `en_US-ryan-medium`, voices directory `~/piper/voices`), keeps a queue, synthesises each line and plays it with `pw-play` (with `XDG_RUNTIME_DIR=/run/user/1000`). Endpoints: `POST /say` with JSON `{text, pace, gap, id, stale_after}` (a line not started by its `stale_after` wall-clock time is dropped, never spoken late); `POST /flush` (drop everything queued and stop what is playing - used when the viewer seeks or changes speed); `GET /status` (alive, voice, queue length). A `--dry-run` flag that writes wavs to a folder instead of playing, so you can test it here.
2. **`bb_call.js`** - the announcers, a new script loaded by `baseball.html` after `bb_game.js`, with no DOM of its own: given the game `G`, the schedule `SEG` and the segment the page is in, it decides what to say and when. Play-by-play: the batter coming up (name, position, how he has done today), the count and the pitch in a few words ("Fastball, ninety-six, high. Ball one."), balls in play called as they happen (built from the play's own description, the fielder, the runners and the score), home runs, steals, wild pitches, pitching changes, the end of each half and the final. Colour commentary in the gaps (half-inning breaks, pitching changes, slow counts): true observations from the data on screen - a hitter's bat speed against the league, a pitcher's pitch count and fatigue, the win probability, the park's altitude and the air. Keep a second voice possible (play-by-play on ryan, colour on lessac) but default to one voice.
3. **The timing budget** - the hard part. The picture does not wait for the voice. Each line must be queued when its moment starts and must FIT the time until the next thing worth saying: estimate its length from your measured table (seconds per word at each pace), shorten or drop lines that will not fit, and give every line a `stale_after` so a late line is dropped rather than spoken over the next pitch. Action lines get the fast pace, colour the slow one. At speeds above 1x, speak only the big moments (or nothing); flush on seek, pause and speed change.
4. **Wiring in `baseball.html`** - minimal: load `bb_call.js`, call it from the frame loop when the segment changes, send its lines to the server with `fetch` (short timeout, all errors swallowed), and add `v` to the key help in the HUD to mute and unmute the announcers. Bump `baseball.html`'s version and CHANGED list.
5. **`headless/call_check.js`** - runs `bb_call.js` over several whole games without a browser and prints the call with its schedule: each line, when it is queued, its estimated length, the time available, and every line dropped or shortened. Report the share of segments that got a line, the share of lines dropped for lack of time, and the longest lag.
