# Brief: bug audit of the game, the bases and the scoring · 2026-10-05

For a cloud Claude Code session (Fable recommended) on `WorldByJoe/baseball-sim`. Written by the local session;
Joe von Fischer asked for it after asking whether some of the open mysteries are bugs. Read this whole brief, then
`CALIBRATION.md`'s last sections (from "U and G, first measurements"). Also read PR #31's description
(`gh pr view 31`), which is the previous cloud session's report.

## Why

**The game loses runs between its events and its scoreboard.** BaseRuns is the standard runs estimator, built from a
team's hits, walks and outs. Applied to the model's own per-team-game totals (Mac, 200 games each), the model scored
only 90-93% of what BaseRuns predicts. MLB 2025 scored 102%.

| | runs | BaseRuns from its own events | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| model v3.0, seeds 3 / 11 / 29 | 3.92 / 4.01 / 4.26 | 4.37 / 4.34 / 4.66 | .898 / .925 / .914 |
| model v3.1, seeds 3 / 11 / 29 | 4.11 / 4.04 / 3.97 | 4.44 / 4.33 / 4.27 | .926 / .933 / .931 |
| MLB 2025 | 4.45 | 4.37 | 1.018 |

(BaseRuns used: A = H + BB + HBP - HR; B = 1.02 (1.4 TB - 0.6 H - 3 HR + 0.1 (BB + HBP)); C = AB - H; D = HR;
runs = A B / (B + C) + D. AB = H / AVG, and HBP is backed out of OBP, ignoring sacrifice flies.)

That is about 0.4 runs a team-game, nearly the whole gap to the league's 4.45.

**Bugs have lived in exactly this layer before:**

- a runner on third vanished when a steal drew a wild throw;
- a runner thrown out on a clean outfield hit was scored a fielder's choice, which robbed the batter of a hit;
- a home run's play lacked the runner list and blanked the screen;
- a third-out catch had the same missing-runner-list shape;
- the hand-set foul ground let fielders catch 2.6 foul pops a team-game against the league's 0.68 outs in foul
  territory (found by the last cloud session; `bb_field.js` v1.1, already on this branch);
- `tools/fit_population.js` silently put refitted hand-set trait means back (fixed on #31 as v0.7; for v3.1's
  traits v0.6's targets are right).

**Other mysteries that may be bugs:**

- BABIP .26 against .291 (hits per band of launch angle were about the league's, so suspect fielding and scoring);
- runners thrown out taking an extra base twice as often as the league's, with caught stealing low and errors 0.36
  against 0.50;
- double plays a little few;
- K% rising with release height where the league's falls (a wrong sign is a classic sign bug);
- ground balls pulled 7 deg against 14.

## What to do

### 1. Measure where the runs leak

Build `headless/runs_check.js`. It plays games through the same path as `headless/run_games.js` (read it first)
and measures the following, against the league measured the same way from the pitch-level data (each plate
appearance's last pitch has `events`, `on_1b/on_2b/on_3b`, `outs_when_up`, `bat_score`/`post_bat_score`, and the
next plate appearance gives the resulting base-out state):

1. **BaseRuns against actual runs** per team-game.
2. **The RE24 table:** runs scored from each of the 24 base-out states to the end of the inning.
3. **Transitions by event and starting state:**
   - single: runner on second scores; runner on first reaches third;
   - double: runner on first scores;
   - fly-ball outs with fewer than two out: runner on third tags and scores (sacrifice-fly rate), runner on second
     advances;
   - ground-ball outs: double plays per chance (runner on first, fewer than two out), runners advancing on outs;
   - outs on the bases per opportunity;
   - wild pitches and passed balls, stolen bases and caught stealing per opportunity.
   - `statcast/baserunning.py` and `headless/xbt_check.js` already measure some of this (league against model);
     reuse and extend them.
4. **Events per team-game, every type:** singles, doubles, triples, home runs, walks, hit by pitch, strikeouts,
   sacrifice flies, double plays, fielder's choices, errors (by kind), outs on the bases, left on base and runners
   left in scoring position.

The leak should then be localised to particular states and events. Report it as a table: model, league, and the
runs it costs.

### 2. Check every simulated play for the impossible

Build `headless/invariants_check.js`. Over thousands of games and every pitch and play it checks:

- **Base states are legal:** no two runners on one base, runners never pass each other, a runner who scores is
  removed, the batter-runner ends where the scoring says.
- **Outs:** never above 3, the inning ends at exactly 3, and no play adds outs after the third.
- **Runs:** every run is credited exactly once and is consistent with the runners who crossed the plate; the score
  is the sum of the runs.
- **Scoring:** the scored result (single, double, fielder's choice, error, sacrifice fly, double play...) is
  consistent with where the batter and runners went and who was out; hits are not lost to fielder's choices or
  errors that the league would score as hits; walk and hit-by-pitch forces are right.
- **Steals, pickoffs, wild pitches and passed balls:** the bases advance correctly, including forced and unforced
  runners.
- **Pitching changes, pinch hitters, defensive changes and double switches** leave nine fielders and a legal
  batting order.

Report every violation with the seed and the play's key so it can be reproduced. Fix each, one commit per bug, and
keep the check as a regression test.

### 3. Mirror and sign tests

- **Left-right mirrors:** a left-handed batter against a right-handed pitcher should mirror a right-handed batter
  against a left-handed pitcher (spray, pull, outcomes). Platoon splits should be about the league's (same-side
  plate appearances roughly 20-30 points of wOBA worse).
- **Sign checks:** the wrong-signed correlations (K% against release height; the flat walk rate against arm angle;
  ground balls pulled 7 deg against 14). Follow each through the code for a flipped sign or a mixed convention:
  - plate x is in feet, from the catcher's view, + toward first base; a right-handed batter stands on -x;
  - Statcast attack_direction is + toward the opposite field;
  - the hit coordinates put home plate at (126.0, 205.0);
  - pfx_x is + toward first base.

### 4. Read the code

Give `bb_game.js` (bases, runners, managers, scoring) and `bb_field.js` (fielders, throws, runners' decisions, the
scorer) a careful fresh review for logic errors:

- off-by-one bases;
- stale per-play state reused across plays;
- runner objects shared by reference;
- branches that return early without the full shape of a play;
- the decision to send or hold a runner against how the throw is resolved.

Check the suspicious paths against the measurements in 1.

## Rules

- **Fix real bugs; do not tune.**
  - A fix must be a correction of logic, or a mechanism measured from the league. Never a knob turned toward runs.
  - If a gap turns out to be a missing mechanism rather than a bug, record it as a hypothesis with its numbers, and
    do not build it in this round unless it is measured and small.
- **Each fix gets its own commit:** what was wrong (file and line), how it showed, the fix, and the regression test
  that failed before and passes after. Report each fix's effect on the suite (runs, the BaseRuns ratio, BABIP, K%,
  BB%, double plays, errors).
- **Code headers:** every changed file keeps `name · vX.Y · date` and a CHANGED list of at most five entries,
  newest first. Delete old code rather than commenting it out; keep comments true to the code.
- **Write it up.** Add a `CALIBRATION.md` section "Bug audit": what was run, the leak table, each bug found and its
  effect, and what is a mechanism rather than a bug. Update the open mysteries and the hypothesis table. Results go
  in the past tense. Regenerate `TRAITS.md` if traits change (`python3 tools/traits_doc.py`). Update
  `docs/trait_map.html` if constants change, and check that it parses and that `N.length` equals the node rows.
- **Branch and PR.** Work on `bug-audit` and open ONE pull request into `breaking-reads`. **Never merge.** End
  commit messages with a Co-Authored-By line for the model you are, and the PR description with
  `🤖 Generated with [Claude Code](https://claude.com/claude-code)`.
- **Hand-back.** The PR gives:
  - the leak table;
  - the bugs found and fixed, with each one's effect;
  - the new line against your baseline;
  - the run-conversion ratio before and after;
  - what is left, with the next suspect.
- **Write in plain language.**
