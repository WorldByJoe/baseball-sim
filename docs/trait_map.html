How a player's traits become the statistics we measure

Every statistic in Ballgame emerges from traits meeting physics, perception and noise. The map runs left to right: who the players are (distal), what they can do on a pitch (proximate), the engine's stages for every pitch with the constants inside them, and the statistics measured against the 2025 league. Each parameter carries a mark for where its value came from. The distal traits are drawn from the POOL of all professional players, majors and minors: the question is that pool's frequency distribution and how it meets the physics. Each major leaguer is the best of six candidates from it, and the next-best play at the levels below, so Triple-A's measurements test the pool from outside the majors.

Hover or tap any box to trace its paths; tap again to release. Tap a class below to light up every parameter of that kind. The ledger under the map lists them all.

Reading it for the joint fit

The amber marks are the knobs that were chosen while watching outcomes; they are the first candidates for the sensitivity analysis. The red marks were set by judgement and nothing yet ties them to a measurement; several of them sit in the batter's eye and plan (eye, pitch spotting, how hard he sits on his guess), the collision's friction and the base-running read, which is where the swing curves by pitch kind, the spray and the spin's shape go wrong. Green, blue and indigo values are tied to measurements or physics and should move only if their own measurement changes. A statistic that no amber or red knob upstream can move points at a missing mechanism.
